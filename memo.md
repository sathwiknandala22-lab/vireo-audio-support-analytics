**Subject:** Support scorecard and replacement-cost signal

## Executive summary
H1 2026 replacement rate was **22.1%**, up from **12.3% in Q4 2025**, while CSAT fell from **3.43 to 3.17**. The strongest operational signal is not a simple agent effect: three Pulse 2 production-lot cohorts (2510, 2511 and 2512) show replacement rates around 34.4–38.4%, versus an approximately 11.0% rate for other Pulse 2 lots.

These cohorts contain **2,428 completed tickets**. Using the policy cost of product unit cost + ₹340 logistics, their excess replacement activity is approximately **637 excess replacements / ₹11.60 lakh** relative to the other-lot baseline. This is an anomaly-based estimate: the ticket data can identify an unusual lot pattern, but it cannot prove a physical manufacturing defect or supplier liability.

## Business goal
**Detect a materially abnormal product lot within about four weeks of its first support tickets, so Operations can investigate containment before the replacement burden compounds.** A historical replay of the monitor crossed its threshold on 26 Nov 2025 for lot 2510, 3 Jan 2026 for 2511, and 12 Feb 2026 for 2512. The separate operational KPI in the dashboard is to reduce the H1 replacement rate from 22.1% to **15%**; at the H1 run-rate, that is about **₹3.38 lakh per quarter** in potential avoided replacement cost.

## Agent view
The requested raw bottom-10 scorecard is available in the dashboard. I treat it as a **review queue**, not a conclusion about individual performance. The raw results are affected by team/channel/issue mix, and Neha's note says hardware-triage work intentionally receives harder cases. Tier 2 Escalations & Warranty is therefore not ranked against Tier 1 for training decisions. A queue-adjusted check is included; it is deliberately presented as a review aid rather than a causal personnel model.

## Suggested next steps
1. Investigate Pulse 2 lots 2510–2512 with Operations/Quality and review remaining inventory and supplier records.
2. Add the lot monitor to a weekly support-operations review, with a minimum-ticket threshold to avoid small-sample noise.
